# Jev question-variant A/B — live results (L6)

Date: 2026-10-04 (run window 2026-10-03 19:06:51–19:23:34 UTC / 00:36–00:53 IST) · mode: **live**
Model: `jev-1.13.0` (echoed identically on all 599 answered calls) · variant under test: **q123-shuffle@v1** (option-order shuffle on all three Jev questions) vs **control@v1**

> **Headline, honestly:** the pre-registered primary comparison finds **no significant difference** between
> variant-flip and noise-flip rates (McNemar p=0.241). But the absolute flip rates (40–47%) do **not**
> measure Jev non-determinism — post-hoc decomposition shows ~89% of the "noise-floor" flips are an artifact
> of a **vendor latency degradation during the run window** (20–24% of calls exceeded the 2700 ms race
> budget and timer-won to `passthrough`). On the 66 alerts where all three arms actually got answers,
> per-question choice-flip rates are **identical** between the noise pair and the variant pair on every
> question — no evidence that option order moves Jev's answers.

## Design (pre-registered 2026-10-04, `research/jev-behavior/ab-math-design-2026-10-04.md`)

- Eval set: n=200 alerts, seed 7, fingerprint `d2da9bccee9d51f8…` — production `build_state`, frozen by
  (n, seed, sha); identical inputs to every arm.
- Arms: A1 = control@v1 (sha `81323e5d48a8…`); A2 = control@v1 (same spec, independent gate/client —
  the within-variant noise floor); B = q123-shuffle@v1 (sha `25699c18f2e9…`). K4 verified pre-live that
  A-questions == production questions bit-for-bit and B-questions differ only in criteria ordering.
- Paired: every alert ran under every arm; per-alert arm order randomized (seeded).
- Kill conditions: K1 (>5% errors / 50 calls), K2 (auth failure), K3 (any 429), K5 (rolling median latency
  > 2400 ms). **None fired.** Zero `error:*` dispositions on any arm (600 calls, 0 errors).
- Rate: per-arm pacer ≤ 0.35 req/s + jitter (as pre-registered). **Correction:** each arm had its own pacer,
  so the observed *global* rate was 600 calls / 1003 s ≈ **0.60 req/s** — still gentle (zero 429s), but the
  run's conditions text as generated says "≤ 0.35 req/s"; the true global number is 0.60. Future runs should
  share one pacer across arms.

### Disposition mix (all arms)

Only two dispositions occurred on this eval set with default thresholds: `page_now` / `passthrough`
(A1 126/74, A2 125/75, B 145/55). B's higher page_now count is fully explained by its fewer timer-wins
(answered calls page more often than timer-won ones do); on the answered-only subset the page_now rates
are A1 84.8% vs B 93.9% (n=66 — see limitations).

## Post-hoc diagnostic 1: the "noise floor" is a latency artifact, not model noise (labeled post-hoc)

Of the 81 A1↔A2 disposition flips, **72 (88.9%) involve a timer-win on at least one side**:
one arm answered (→ `page_now`/`passthrough` by policy) while the other exceeded 2700 ms (→ forced
`passthrough`). Same for 77/93 (82.8%) of A1↔B flips. The pre-registered 40.5% "noise floor" therefore
measures **vendor latency variance against a fixed race budget**, not Jev answer non-determinism.
This is a limitation of the *metric definition under degradation*, not of the paired design — and it is
itself a finding: at B=2700 ms, tonight's tail turned one alert in four into a timer-win, and every
timer-win is a disposition the model never got to make.

## Post-hoc diagnostic 2: answered-only subset (n=66, exploratory — not pre-registered)

Alerts where all three arms received real answers (no timer-win anywhere):

| comparison | n | flips | flip rate | Wilson 95% CI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| noise (A1 vs A2) | 66 | 9 | 0.1364 | [0.0734, 0.2393] |
| variant (A1 vs B) | 66 | 16 | 0.2424 | [0.1551, 0.3581] |

Per-question **choice** flips on this subset — noise vs variant:

| question | noise A1↔A2 | variant A1↔B | note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| severity | 2/66 = 0.0303 [0.0083, 0.1039] | 2/66 = 0.0303 | **identical alert set** — flips are alert-intrinsic (boundary cases), not variant-driven |
| owning_team | 0/66 = 0.0000 | 0/66 = 0.0000 | no flips at all |
| disposition | 10/66 = 0.1515 [0.0844, 0.2569] | 10/66 = 0.1515 | 6/10 alerts overlap; equal counts, no directional shift |

Reading: **option order does not move Jev's choices.** On every question the variant pair flips exactly as
often as the identical-repeat pair — for severity, on literally the same two alerts. The disposition
question is the noisiest of the three (15.2% repeat choice-flip, CI [8.4%, 25.7%] — above the researched
1.3–2.2% general band; severity at 3.0% sits at its edge). The money question is the least stable one,
which is direct support for the product's confidence-gating thesis: you cannot trust the raw choice.

## What this means (and doesn't)

1. **Option-ordering is not a lever.** q123-shuffle@v1 (criteria order shuffled on all 3 questions) shows
   no behavioral effect beyond the noise floor, on choices or dispositions. The CI repeatability probes'
   <3% order-shuffle bar is consistent with what we see; UI/design is free to order options for humans.
2. **The disposition question needs the gating, not the wording.** 15.2% repeat choice-flip on Q3 vs 3.0%
   on Q1 says instability lives in the hardest judgment, not the prompt surface. Optimization effort goes
   to calibration/thresholds, not rewording.
3. **Latency variance dominates reliability tonight, not model noise.** 24% timer-win at B=2700 ms with
   zero errors: the vendor answers everything eventually, just with a heavy time-varying tail. The race
   budget B is doing real work — without it, p99 decisions would take 4+ s.
4. **Not shown:** whether any variant *improves* decisions (no outcome labels — flips are sensitivity, not
   quality); production alert text (synthetic fixtures); any other variant (q3-suppress-hedge-drop-v1 is
   built, versioned, and ready to run).

## HONEST LIMITATIONS

1. **Flip magnitude is not flip quality.** Without outcome labels on the eval alerts, a variant that flips
   dispositions may flip toward or away from correct. This harness measures behavioral sensitivity, not
   improvement.
2. **The headline 40.5%/46.5% flip rates are latency artifacts** (diagnostic 1). Do not cite them as "Jev
   flips 40% of the time." The answered-only rates (13.6%/24.2%, n=66) are closer to behavioral noise but
   are a post-hoc subset — selection effects (slow/hard alerts excluded) are unquantified.
3. **Synthetic alerts.** The eval set is seeded fixtures; real customer alert text may be more or less
   order-sensitive, and confidence coverage will differ.
4. **Single time window.** Vendor behavior drifts — tonight's degraded tail (p99 ~4.3 s) vs yesterday's
   healthy window (p99 1526 ms) proves one night's noise floor is not a standing constant. Re-run the
   A1/A2 calibration alongside every future variant.
5. **n=200 is underpowered** for flip deltas under ~3pp at the measured noise floor — the CIs are the
   finding, not the p-value. The answered-only n=66 is weaker still.
6. **Rate correction:** observed global rate was 0.60 req/s (per-arm pacers), not the ≤0.35 the generated
   conditions text states. No 429s were observed; K3 never approached firing.
